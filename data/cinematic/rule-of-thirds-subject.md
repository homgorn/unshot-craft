Cinematic composition of [Subject] placed on the left third, looking into open
negative space on the right, eye line on the upper horizontal, background uncluttered
and darker than the subject, 35mm, rule of thirds.

## Lede

A subject on a third line, looking into the space it occupies, is a frame that
already reads as a frame before anything happens in it. The negative space is not
empty: it is the room the shot is going to move into.

## When to use

Establishing shots, copy-led layouts, and any frame where you will later place text
or a product UI. The space you leave is the space you will need.

## Directing the AI

Say which third and which direction the subject looks. "Off-centre, negative space
on the right" is enough; "rule of thirds" alone gets you a centred subject, because
that is the model's default reading of a plain product shot.

## Common mistakes

- Centring everything, because "cinematic composition" biases toward symmetry.
- Filling the negative space with objects, which leaves nowhere to breathe.
- Horizon tangent to the subject's head, a classic tangency error.
