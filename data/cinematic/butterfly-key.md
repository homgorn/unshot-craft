Studio portrait of [Subject] under a butterfly key: the main light raised high and
tilted down so its shadow falls under the nose, a low reflector bouncing fill back
under the chin, black background, glamour lighting, 85mm, crisp catchlights.

## Lede

Moving the key above the subject's eye line and tilting it down puts the nose
shadow under the nose rather than to one side. That single geometric change flattens
the face symmetrically, which is why fashion and beauty lighting has used it for a
century.

## When to use

Beauty and cosmetics work, skincare texture shots, any frontal hero image where
symmetry is the point. Not for interviews: the symmetrical shadow reads as
artificial in conversation.

## Directing the AI

Say "high key light tilted down, nose shadow falls beneath the nose, low bounce
reflector under the chin". The bounce is what keeps it from going harsh — without it
the under-chin area goes black and the face reads as a mask.

## Common mistakes

- The shadow lands to the side, so the AI placed the key at eye level. Name the
  height explicitly.
- Omitting the bounce and getting a harsh, dark underside.
- Flash-on-the-lens look: a frontal catchlight instead of two angled ones.
